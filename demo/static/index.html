UWA Honours research · In progress

From signing
to understanding.

Investigating gloss-free Auslan sign language to speech translation.

How far can better visual representations and carefully constructed training examples take translation when annotated data is scarce?

Explore the translator ↗Read the findings ↓
Preet PrasadThe University of Western AustraliaSupervised by Dr Atif Mansoor

One language. A distinct challenge. Auslan is the language of the Australian Deaf community, with its own grammar and rich visual expression.

Our focus is translation from conversational signing, using movement coordinates paired with English sentences.

01 / Evidence, with context

Progress is measurable.
Generalisability is the test.

The official benchmark tells only part of the story. Explore performance on all test sentences and on sentences whose English targets were absent from training.

28.75%

of test targets also occur in training.

230 of 800 English reference sentences are familiar. The ensemble scores 35.00 on these targets and 6.60 on unseen targets.

What the evidence supports

The ensemble improves on image fusion by 1.467 BLEU-4, with a paired-bootstrap 95% interval of +0.396 to +2.563.

The two earlier single-model increments are not individually distinguishable from zero under the same analysis.

01

Visual input matters.

Controlled comparisons identify movement tracking as a major source of improvement. The translator can only learn from the visual signal it receives.

02

More data is not enough.

Synthetic sequences connect isolated signs to words. Matching their timing to conversational signing improved the mean gain to 1.48 BLEU-4 across three paired runs.

03

Recognition remains difficult.

Word-level analysis found no correct content words in about 70% of clips across three single models. A higher benchmark score does not imply reliable conversation.

02 / From evidence to interaction

A complete utterance.
A considered response.

The interface follows the model’s actual capabilities: capture a short utterance, translate after Finish, review the English, then choose whether to speak it.

  1. 01 / CAPTURE

    Signing video

    Camera enabled by the user. One signer, hands and upper body in view.

  2. 02 / REPRESENT

    Tracked movement

    DWPose extracts 133 body, face and hand points for each captured frame.

  3. 03 / TRANSLATE

    English text

    A pose-only Transformer generates a sentence after the utterance ends.

  4. 04 / RESPOND

    Optional speech

    Review, copy or read aloud using an available local English voice.

Confidence guides whether to respond. An experimental threshold can withhold uncertain output and request repetition. It is not a calibrated measure of correctness.

03 / Scope & responsibility

Make the limits
as visible as the results.

This is an active research system. The interface is designed for public access; the model’s live translation quality still requires evaluation with Auslan signers.

Offline results ≠ webcam performance

The headline result uses six models on stored benchmark clips. The live interface uses one pose-only model. New signers, camera conditions and capture timing can change its behaviour.

People remain in control

Camera access is explicit. Recording ends when you select Finish or reach the ten-second limit. Uncertain translations may be withheld. This system cannot replace an Auslan interpreter.

Temporary data, by design

Captured frames are sent to the server during recording and processing. Images are not saved. Capture buffers are released after completion, cancellation or disconnection; an in-flight computation may finish first. Conversation text stays in the current browser page.

Evaluation includes the interaction

The next study needs new webcam recordings and signer feedback, measuring translation quality, the proportion of utterances answered, capture timing and delay before speech.
